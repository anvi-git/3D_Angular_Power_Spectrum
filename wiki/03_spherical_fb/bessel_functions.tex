\subsection{Spherical Bessel Functions}\label{subsec:sfb_bessel}

This section introduces spherical Bessel functions, which form the radial basis for 3D field decompositions in spherical coordinates.

\subsubsection{Definition and properties}

The spherical Bessel function of order $\ell$ is defined by:

\begin{equation}
j_\ell(x) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}} J_{\ell+1/2}(x)
\end{equation}

where $J_\nu$ is the Cylindrical Bessel function. Alternatively, a recurrence relation defines them:

\begin{equation}
j_\ell(x) = \frac{\ell}{x} j_{\ell-1}(x) - j'_{\ell-1}(x)
\end{equation}

with initial conditions:
\begin{align}
j_0(x) &= \frac{\sin x}{x}\\
j_1(x) &= \frac{\sin x}{x^2} - \frac{\cos x}{x}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Asymptotic behavior}

- **Small argument** ($x \ll \ell$): $j_\ell(x) \approx \frac{x^\ell}{(2\ell+1)!!}$
- **Large argument** ($x \gg \ell$): $j_\ell(x) \approx \frac{\sin(x - \ell\pi/2)}{x}$

\subsubsection{Zeros and roots}

The $n$-th zero of $j_\ell(x)$ is denoted $x_{\ell,n}$. These are essential for defining SFB radial grids.

\example{
For $\ell=0$: $x_{0,1} = \pi, x_{0,2} = 2\pi, ...$

For $\ell=1$: $x_{1,1} \approx 4.49, x_{1,2} \approx 7.73, ...$
}

See \cref{subsec:sfb_expansion} for use in defining the SFB radial grid.
