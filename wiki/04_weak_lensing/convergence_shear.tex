\subsection{Convergence and Shear: Scalar and Tensor Fields}\label{subsec:wl_convergence_shear}

This section defines the weak lensing fields and their physical interpretation.

\subsubsection{Convergence: isotropic magnification}

The convergence $\kappa$ is the isotropic magnification/demagnification caused by lensing:

\begin{equation}
\kappa = \frac{1}{2}\nabla^2_\perp \psi
\end{equation}

where $\nabla^2_\perp$ is the 2D Laplacian on the sphere.

$\kappa > 0$ means magnification (objects appear brighter), $\kappa < 0$ means demagnification.

**Physical meaning**: $\kappa$ measures the integrated projected density along the line of sight.

\subsubsection{Shear: tidal distortion}

The shear $\gamma$ is the anisotropic tidal distortion of source images:

\begin{equation}
\gamma_1 + i\gamma_2 = \left(\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta^2} - \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\phi^2}\right) \psi - i \frac{2}{\sin\theta} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta \partial\phi} \psi
\end{equation}

This is a spin-$\pm 2$ tensor field (shear stretches images in two perpendicular directions).

\subsubsection{E-modes and B-modes}

Shear decomposes into:
- **E-modes**: Curl-free (gradient-like) component → pure from gravitational lensing
- **B-modes**: Divergence-free (curl-like) component → zero for pure lensing (systematics diagnostic)

See \cref{subsec:wl_spin_harmonics} for harmonic decomposition.
